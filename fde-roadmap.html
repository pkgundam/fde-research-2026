1 · What an FDE does

The loop every posting describes, in one form or another.

  1. 1DiscoverSit with the customer; find the workflow that actually hurts.
  2. 2ScopeTurn the pain into a bounded, measurable first deliverable.
  3. 3PrototypeShip something rough in days, on their data.
  4. 4IntegrateWire it into the systems they already run.
  5. 5DeployGet it into their environment, under their constraints.
  6. 6EvaluateMeasure against the outcome you scoped, not vibes.
  7. 7ImproveClose the gaps the eval exposed.
  8. 8Feed backBring what the field taught you to the product team.

3 · Frequency vs criticality

Frequency (x): how often a skill appears. Criticality (y): the share of its mentions tagged as a responsibility ("what you will do") rather than a requirement — i.e. how often the skill is the job. Faded points have fewer than 5 mentions.

Top-left — Hidden core: rarely listed, but when listed it is the work. Top-right — The job: common and central. Learn these first. Bottom-left — Peripheral: occasional nice-to-haves. Bottom-right — Table stakes: everyone asks; nobody hires for it alone.

5 · Learning roadmap

Staged tracks ordered by what the data says matters first.

Python for FDE

Skip (for now): Go (12% of postings ask for it) · C / C++ (8%) · Rust (5%) · Competitive-programming algorithm drills (4%) · Async/concurrency internals (under 1%) · Deep CPython internals, metaclasses, and packaging minutiae (not in the taxonomy; no posting text mentioned them)
  1. Stage 1 — Language & tooling — PythonGit & code reviewTypeScript / JavaScript
    Python is asked for in 55% of postings — the near-universal baseline — but only 11% of those mentions frame it as the responsibility itself rather than a checkbox. Git follows at 11% asked / 56% responsibility, and TypeScript at 35% asked / 15% responsibility.
  2. Stage 2 — Working with real data — REST / API integrationData modelling & schemasSQL & relational DBs
    REST/API integration is asked for in 38% of postings and is the responsibility 50% of the time it appears — the clearest sign this is integration work, not algorithm work. Data modelling (9% asked / 47% responsibility) and SQL (20% asked / 18% responsibility) round out the stage.
  3. Stage 3 — Production quality — System designDebugging & troubleshootingTesting
    System design is asked for in 59% of postings and framed as a responsibility 60% of the time; debugging follows at 39% asked / 55% responsibility. Testing is asked for far less often (15%) but when it appears it is usually the job itself (64% responsibility).

AI application engineering

  1. Stage 1 — Core LLM application skills — Agents & tool useLLM APIsPrompt engineering
    Agents & tool use is asked for in 48% of postings and is the responsibility 48% of the time — close to a coin flip between knowing about agents and building them. LLM APIs lead on raw frequency (55%) but skew more toward requirements (37% responsibility); prompt engineering is asked for far less often (14%) but is the responsibility 46% of the time it appears.
  2. Stage 2 — Retrieval & orchestration — RAG & retrievalLLM frameworksVector databases
    RAG is asked for in 17% of postings and is the responsibility 30% of the time — a smaller, more specialised slice than the core LLM-API skills above. Orchestration frameworks such as LangChain (10% asked) and vector databases (6% asked) are rarer still.
  3. Stage 3 — Quality & production — Evals & quality measurementGuardrails & safetyModel serving & inferenceLLM observability & tracing
    Evals are asked for in 33% of postings and are the responsibility 48% of the time. Guardrails & safety is rarer (15% asked) but skews more toward being the actual job (63% responsibility); model serving (20% asked / 31% responsibility) and LLM observability (7% asked / 54% responsibility) round out the stage.

Integration & deployment

  1. Stage 1 — Wire it into their systems — Data pipelines & ETLEnterprise systems (ERP/CRM)Auth & identity
    Data pipelines are asked for in 22% of postings and are the responsibility 41% of the time; integrating with enterprise systems such as Salesforce, SAP or ServiceNow sits close behind at 15% asked / 43% responsibility. Auth & identity integration is asked for in 12% of postings.
  2. Stage 2 — Run it in their environment — On-prem / air-gapped deploymentCI/CDAWSKubernetesDocker & containers
    On-prem / air-gapped deployment is asked for in 16% of postings and is the responsibility 53% of the time — a customer-controlled environment, not a managed cloud, is the norm here. AWS alone is asked for in 26% of postings, ahead of Kubernetes (18%) and Docker (12%); CI/CD is asked for in 15% of postings and is the responsibility 28% of the time.
  3. Stage 3 — Keep it running and compliant — Performance & cost optimisationSecurity & complianceObservability & monitoringIncident response & on-call
    Cost & performance optimisation is asked for in 40% of postings and is the responsibility 64% of the time — the strongest 'own this' signal in the cluster. Security & compliance follows at 36% asked / 50% responsibility, observability at 24% asked / 57% responsibility, and incident response & on-call at 17% asked / 53% responsibility.

Customer delivery & communication

  1. Stage 1 — Own the engagement — Implementation & onboardingScoping & discoveryCustomer-facing engineering
    Implementation & onboarding is asked for in 58% of postings and is the responsibility 82% of the time — one of the highest responsibility shares in the data. Scoping & discovery is asked for in 56% of postings (76% responsibility), and customer-facing work itself is asked for in 78% of postings.
  2. Stage 2 — Communicate under pressure — Cross-functional collaborationStakeholder communicationWritten communication
    Cross-functional collaboration is asked for in 61% of postings and is the responsibility 70% of the time. Stakeholder communication follows at 52% asked / 50% responsibility, and written communication at 38% asked / 58% responsibility.
  3. Stage 3 — Close the loop — Feeding insight back to productTravel & on-site workTraining & enablement
    Feeding field learning back to product is asked for in 57% of postings and is the responsibility 92% of the time — the highest responsibility share of any skill in the taxonomy. Travel or on-site work (43% asked / 60% responsibility) and training & enablement (30% asked / 81% responsibility) round out the loop.

Startup vs enterprise

This comparison is restricted to full ATS job descriptions; the Hacker News listings are excluded because they are short and rarely spell out a skill list the way a formal posting does. Within that restricted set, 122 postings carry a startup or scaleup size hint and 56 carry an enterprise hint. Enterprise postings are several times more likely to ask for Java and for structured data-pipeline work, and more likely to mention cross-functional collaboration. Startup postings mention rapid prototyping about twice as often, while enterprise postings ask more often for cost and performance optimisation and for travel or on-site work. Both sample sizes are thin enough that these differences should be read as directional, not precise.

8 · Methodology

n = 303 unique postings from 154 companies, posted 2022-06-30 to 2026-09-14. ATS postings were live on 2026-09-15; Hacker News entries come from the 10 monthly "Who is hiring?" threads from 2025-12 to 2026-09 (a company's monthly reposts were merged). 91% of postings were first published within the 12 months before collection (ATS boards report original publish dates). Deduplicated on (normalised company, normalised title); postings sharing that key were merged only when their texts overlapped by at least 80% on 6-word shingles (containment).

Fetched = jobs listed on the board (ATS), comment hits across queries (HN), or rows scanned (Arbeitnow).

Extraction. Each posting was segmented into responsibilities / requirements / nice-to-have sections (230 by explicit headers, 73 inferred) and passed once, with the full closed taxonomy, to claude-sonnet-5 (Claude Code `sonnet` alias, run as subagents rather than through the API) using a fixed prompt. Every returned skill was validated against the taxonomy; 0 out-of-vocabulary mentions were logged and reviewed. Adzuna: not used. LinkedIn and Indeed were not scraped.

Limitations.

  • Sample skews toward companies with public ATS boards and HN presence.
  • HN postings have weaker structure; company size for them is assumed startup.
  • Extraction is LLM-based; evidence quotes are provided but not human-verified for every posting.
  • Startup-vs-enterprise comparisons are directional only.
  • 73 of 303 postings had no explicit section headers, so responsibility/requirement tags were inferred rather than read directly from the posting's structure.
  • 33 postings yielded no requirement-section mentions at all.
  • ATS boards report a posting's original publish date, not when it was collected; roughly 9% of postings are older than 12 months.
  • Company size hints (startup / scaleup / enterprise) come from a hand-maintained lookup by company name, not a verified source.
