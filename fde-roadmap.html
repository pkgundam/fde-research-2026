Key findings
  1. Customer-facing engineering is the most requested skill of all, in 78% of postings. Implementation & onboarding (59%), feeding insight back to product (58%) and scoping (55%) are framed as the job itself 74–91% of the time they appear.
  2. Python is table stakes, not the job. Asked for in 53% of postings, but only 8% of its mentions describe what you will actually do; TypeScript (33% / 12%) and verbal communication (48% / 19%) follow the same pattern.
  3. The AI core is LLM APIs and agents. LLM APIs appear in 55% of postings and agents & tool use in 50%; RAG in 17% and vector databases in 6%. Evals appear in a third.
  4. Eight skills asked for in at least 40% of postings are taught by no standard AI-engineer roadmap: customer-facing work, implementation, product feedback, scoping, stakeholder communication, travel, rapid prototyping and expectation management.
  5. This is a senior, on-site role. 49% of postings are senior level, 46% rate customer interaction at the maximum, and 31% expect frequent travel. Enterprise postings ask for Java (41% vs 5%) and data pipelines (50% vs 11%) far more than startups, which lean on prototyping (57% vs 34%).

1 · What an FDE does

The loop every posting describes, in one form or another.

  1. 1DiscoverSit with the customer; find the workflow that actually hurts.
  2. 2ScopeTurn the pain into a bounded, measurable first deliverable.
  3. 3PrototypeShip something rough in days, on their data.
  4. 4IntegrateWire it into the systems they already run.
  5. 5DeployGet it into their environment, under their constraints.
  6. 6EvaluateMeasure against the outcome you scoped, not vibes.
  7. 7ImproveClose the gaps the eval exposed.
  8. 8Feed backBring what the field taught you to the product team.

3 · Frequency vs criticality

Frequency (x): how often a skill appears. Criticality (y): the share of its mentions tagged as a responsibility ("what you will do") rather than a requirement — i.e. how often the skill is the job — computed on postings with explicit section headers. Skills with fewer than 5 mentions on that basis are omitted from the chart. The quadrant split is at the median on each axis, so by construction roughly a quarter of skills land in each quadrant. Muted chips appear in fewer than 20 postings.

5 · Learning roadmap

Staged tracks ordered by what the data says matters first.

Python for FDE

Skip (for now): Go (11% of postings ask for it) · C / C++ (6%) · Rust (5%) · Competitive-programming algorithm drills (2%) · Async/concurrency internals (under 1%) · Deep CPython internals, metaclasses, and packaging minutiae (not in the taxonomy; no posting text mentioned them)
  1. Stage 1 — Language & tooling — PythonGit & code reviewTypeScript / JavaScript
    Python is asked for in 53% of postings — the near-universal baseline — but only 8% of those mentions frame it as the responsibility itself rather than a checkbox. Git follows at 11% asked / 54% responsibility, and TypeScript at 33% asked / 12% responsibility.
  2. Stage 2 — Working with real data — REST / API integrationSQL & relational DBsData modelling & schemas
    REST/API integration is asked for in 38% of postings and is the responsibility 48% of the time it appears — the clearest sign this is integration work, not algorithm work. Data modelling (8% asked / 46% responsibility) and SQL (20% asked / 18% responsibility) round out the stage.
  3. Stage 3 — Production quality — System designDebugging & troubleshootingTesting
    System design is asked for in 57% of postings and framed as a responsibility 55% of the time; debugging follows at 38% asked / 56% responsibility. Testing is asked for far less often (15%) but when it appears it is usually the job itself (62% responsibility).

AI application engineering

  1. Stage 1 — Core LLM application skills — Agents & tool useLLM APIsPrompt engineering
    Agents & tool use is asked for in 50% of postings and is the responsibility 43% of the time — close to a coin flip between knowing about agents and building them. LLM APIs lead on raw frequency (55%) but skew more toward requirements (34% responsibility); prompt engineering is asked for far less often (13%) but is the responsibility 43% of the time it appears.
  2. Stage 2 — Retrieval & orchestration — RAG & retrievalLLM frameworksVector databases
    RAG is asked for in 17% of postings and is the responsibility 30% of the time — a smaller, more specialised slice than the core LLM-API skills above. Orchestration frameworks such as LangChain (10% asked) and vector databases (6% asked) are rarer still.
  3. Stage 3 — Quality & production — Evals & quality measurementGuardrails & safetyModel serving & inferenceLLM observability & tracing
    Evals are asked for in 33% of postings and are the responsibility 48% of the time. Guardrails & safety is rarer (15% asked) but skews more toward being the actual job (60% responsibility); model serving (20% asked / 28% responsibility) and LLM observability (7% asked / 52% responsibility) round out the stage.

Integration & deployment

  1. Stage 1 — Wire it into their systems — Data pipelines & ETLEnterprise systems (ERP/CRM)Auth & identity
    Data pipelines are asked for in 20% of postings and are the responsibility 40% of the time; integrating with enterprise systems such as Salesforce, SAP or ServiceNow sits close behind at 14% asked / 42% responsibility. Auth & identity integration is asked for in 13% of postings.
  2. Stage 2 — Run it in their environment — On-prem / air-gapped deploymentCI/CDAWSKubernetesDocker & containers
    On-prem / air-gapped deployment is asked for in 16% of postings and is the responsibility 49% of the time — a customer-controlled environment, not a managed cloud, is the norm here. AWS alone is asked for in 25% of postings, ahead of Kubernetes (18%) and Docker (11%); CI/CD is asked for in 15% of postings and is the responsibility 26% of the time.
  3. Stage 3 — Keep it running and compliant — Performance & cost optimisationSecurity & complianceObservability & monitoringIncident response & on-call
    Cost & performance optimisation is asked for in 39% of postings and is the responsibility 60% of the time — the strongest 'own this' signal in the cluster. Security & compliance follows at 35% asked / 47% responsibility, observability at 24% asked / 57% responsibility, and incident response & on-call at 16% asked / 49% responsibility.

Customer delivery & communication

  1. Stage 1 — Own the engagement — Implementation & onboardingScoping & discoveryCustomer-facing engineering
    Implementation & onboarding is asked for in 59% of postings and is the responsibility 79% of the time — one of the highest responsibility shares in the data. Scoping & discovery is asked for in 55% of postings (74% responsibility), and customer-facing work itself is asked for in 78% of postings.
  2. Stage 2 — Communicate under pressure — Cross-functional collaborationStakeholder communicationWritten communication
    Cross-functional collaboration is asked for in 59% of postings and is the responsibility 72% of the time. Stakeholder communication follows at 53% asked / 48% responsibility, and written communication at 38% asked / 59% responsibility.
  3. Stage 3 — Close the loop — Feeding insight back to productTraining & enablementTravel & on-site work
    Feeding field learning back to product is asked for in 58% of postings and is the responsibility 91% of the time — the highest responsibility share of any skill in the taxonomy. Training & enablement (32% asked / 78% responsibility) and travel or on-site work (41% asked / 59% responsibility) round out the loop.

Startup vs enterprise

This comparison is restricted to full ATS job descriptions; the Hacker News listings are excluded because they are short and rarely spell out a skill list the way a formal posting does. Within that restricted set, 111 postings carry a startup or scaleup size hint and 44 carry an enterprise hint. Enterprise postings are several times more likely to ask for Java and for structured data-pipeline work, and more likely to mention cross-functional collaboration. Startup postings mention rapid prototyping about 1.7 times as often, while enterprise postings ask more often for cost and performance optimisation and for travel or on-site work. Both sample sizes are thin enough that these differences should be read as directional, not precise.

8 · Methodology

303 postings were collected; 27 first published before 2025-09-15 were excluded, leaving n = 276 posted within that window (2025-09-16 to 2026-09-14), from 151 companies. ATS postings were live on 2026-09-15; Hacker News entries come from the 10 monthly "Who is hiring?" threads from 2025-12 to 2026-09 (a company's monthly reposts were merged). Deduplicated on (normalised company, normalised title); postings sharing that key were merged only when their texts overlapped by at least 80% on 6-word shingles (containment).

Fetched = jobs listed on the board (ATS), comment hits across queries (HN), or rows scanned (Arbeitnow).

Extraction. Each posting was segmented into responsibilities / requirements / nice-to-have sections (204 by explicit headers, 72 inferred) and passed once, with the full closed taxonomy, to claude-sonnet-5 (Claude Code `sonnet` alias, run as subagents rather than through the API) using a fixed prompt. Criticality is computed on the 204 postings with explicit section headers; computed on all 276 postings instead, the skill ranking is nearly identical (Spearman ρ = 0.987, 2 of 79 skills change quadrant). Extraction was restricted to the taxonomy, so the validator only checks format. To estimate what the taxonomy misses, a separate open-vocabulary pass on 30 randomly sampled postings named skills freely; 88% of the 1143 phrases mapped to a taxonomy node. Most frequent unmapped: forward deployed engineering · ambiguity navigation · backend engineering · full-stack development · claude code · github copilot · cursor · field engineering. Adzuna: not used. LinkedIn and Indeed were not scraped.

Limitations.

  • Sample skews toward companies with public ATS boards and HN presence.
  • HN postings have weaker structure; company size for them is assumed startup.
  • Extraction is LLM-based; evidence quotes are provided but not human-verified for every posting.
  • Startup-vs-enterprise comparisons are directional only.
  • 72 of 276 postings had no explicit section headers, so responsibility/requirement tags were inferred rather than read directly from the posting's structure.
  • 33 postings yielded no requirement-section mentions at all.
  • Only postings first published in the 12 months before collection are included; 27 older postings were dropped.
  • Company size hints (startup / scaleup / enterprise) come from a hand-maintained lookup by company name, not a verified source.
  • Criticality is computed on header-segmented postings only (see Methodology).
